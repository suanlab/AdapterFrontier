\begin{abstract}
Parameter-efficient fine-tuning leaves practitioners with populations of adapters, and recent work reports that combining them improves accuracy and calibration. We ask what combining buys against a strong single adapter. First, an exploratory audit of a $1{,}028$-cell corpus ($67$ pools, $10$ base models, $8$ tasks), registered at git SHA \texttt{02a5a6bc} before any adapter was trained\footnote{Cell count: $1{,}028 = 827$ v1.0 main-pool cells (55 pools $\times$ ensemble methods $\times$ \{\texttt{best\_of\_n}, \texttt{n\_rank}\} $\times$ \{accuracy, ECE\}) $+$ $100$ v1.1 HellaSwag multichoice cells $+$ $100$ baseline self-comparison cells $+$ $1$ Phase-0 smoke-test cell. The $2$ v1.1 GSM8K and $6$ v1.2 supplementary pools (App.~\ref{app:prereg}) are reported separately in Table~\ref{tab:v11cells} and are not in the headline $1{,}028$.} but with headline slices chosen after the data were seen: against a better-resourced single, encoder ensembling never significantly improves accuracy ($0$ of $92$ cells; $29\%$ significantly worse) and decoder ensembling sometimes does ($13$ of $100$, all MNLI), though pools sharing baselines leave only $10$ and $15$ independent clusters. Second, confirmatory experiments, each registered before its runs, on two new tasks with exactly matched training budgets and sealed test sets: an ensemble of rank-$8$ adapters loses to the best rank-$32$ single on BERT-base ($-1.0$ to $-2.8$pp) and BERT-large, and beats it on Qwen2.5-0.5B ($+1.0$ to $+3.2$pp) and SmolLM2-360M, also after temperature-scaling both sides, and the split survives tuning either side within the budget. Post hoc, averaging helps both families slightly at identical runs; what flips by family is what the budget buys a single adapter. Both decoders are at most $0.5$B, and an $8$-member ensemble costs $11$--$18\times$ a merged single's measured latency. Comparing against the pool's best member instead of the stronger single triples the SUPPORTED rate ($6.8\%\to21.9\%$). We release the cells, the experiments' member logits, and code to evaluate new combination rules on them.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) with LoRA-style adapters \citep{hu2021lora} has made it routine to keep dozens of task-specific adapters around: serving stacks such as vLLM and S-LoRA \citep{kwon2023efficient,sheng2024slora} route per request to one of $N$ adapters at negligible extra cost, and frameworks such as LoRAHub \citep{huang2023lorahub} target multi-adapter composition directly. The practitioner's question is whether \emph{combining} those adapters --- averaging weights, voting on labels, or routing through them --- beats spending the same budget on a single adapter. Recent work reports that it does, in accuracy and in calibration \citep{wang2023loraensembles,balabanov2024uncertainty,muhlematter2024loraensemble}. We ask a prior question: \emph{how much of that reported benefit survives a strict evaluation protocol?}

Answering this requires comparisons fixed before the data was seen, because the choices involved --- which single adapter counts as the baseline, how it was selected, at what budget, on which benchmark --- are the choices that determine the answer. We take two steps. First, an \emph{audit}, exploratory in its headline slices (chosen after the data were seen): we pre-registered \textsc{AdapterFrontier} at git SHA \texttt{02a5a6bc} before any adapter was trained and adjudicate each (pool, rule, baseline, metric) cell with paired bootstrap CIs and per-arm Benjamini--Hochberg FDR \citep{bh1995}. Second, because the audit's baseline is better-resourced rather than budget-matched and its member logits were not kept, \emph{confirmatory experiments}, each registered before its runs, that matches training budgets exactly, calibrates both sides and is scored on sealed test sets.

\paragraph{What the audit finds.} Its \texttt{n\_rank} single received a median $1.45\times$ the pool's training compute plus a hyperparameter search, so it is a better-resourced competitor, not a matched one. Against it, \textbf{no encoder cell shows a significant accuracy gain} ($0$ of $92$) while $29\%$ are significantly worse; on decoders $13$ of $100$ cells are SUPPORTED, none REVERSED, all on MNLI. These counts overstate the independent evidence: pools sharing a baseline and test set leave $10$ encoder and $15$ decoder clusters, at which level the encoder mean is ${-}1.20$pp ($95\%$ CI $[{-}2.30,{-}0.37]$) and the decoder mean ${+}0.66$pp ($[{-}0.09,{+}1.37]$). Ensembles improve ECE in $51$ of the same $92$ encoder cells, but against a single that was never calibrated, and the corpus kept too few member logits to supply the control (\S\ref{sec:findings}).

\paragraph{What the experiment finds.} On SNLI and Yahoo Answers, neither in the corpus, at budgets of $4$--$16$ epochs with identical training tokens on both sides, an ensemble of rank-$8$ adapters loses to the best rank-$32$ single on BERT-base ($-1.0$ to $-2.8$pp) and beats it on Qwen2.5-0.5B ($+1.0$ to $+3.2$pp), as registered, and a size-matched pair (BERT-large, SmolLM2-360M) shows the same split, so across four backbones it tracks family rather than size; after one temperature per arm the decoder ensemble's NLL is still lower and the encoder ensemble's higher. Letting the single tune rank and learning rate inside the same budget leaves BERT unchanged and narrows the Qwen gap to $+1.1$/$+1.7$pp; no Qwen configuration reaches the ensemble even when picked on test (\S\ref{sec:a3}). The decoder gain costs $11$--$18\times$ a merged single's measured latency at $N{=}8$.

\paragraph{Three shortcuts move the verdict.} Selecting the ``best single'' on test is worth $+1.12$pp to whichever arm may peek. On the same $48$ pools, comparing against the pool's best member rather than the stronger single triples the SUPPORTED rate ($6.8\%\to21.9\%$) and cuts REVERSED from $14.1\%$ to $3.6\%$. And MNLI's $-0.012$pp task mean reads as ``no effect'' while concealing $30$ significant cells pointing both ways (\S\ref{sec:protocol}).

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\textbf{(i)~A pre-registered corpus and a reusable subset}: $1{,}028$ cell JSONs ($927$ valid) with per-cell CIs, $p$-values and verdicts that re-adjudicate from released predictions, and $280$ new runs (the $264$ of \S\ref{sec:a3}, a $12$-run pilot and $4$ learning-rate checks) whose per-member validation and test logits are in the anonymous release, on which a new combination rule is evaluated end to end without retraining (\texttt{examples/\allowbreak new\_\allowbreak combiner\_\allowbreak demo.\allowbreak py}).
\textbf{(ii)~An audit against a better-resourced single}, with the dependence structure stated: an encoder null with frequent harm, a narrow decoder gain.
\textbf{(iii)~A pre-registered equal-budget test} on four backbones that confirms the family split in accuracy and in calibrated NLL, separates it from model size, survives a tuned single, and prices the gain in measured latency.
\textbf{(iv)~A protocol-sensitivity analysis} pricing three shortcuts that each move the verdict (Table~\ref{tab:protocol}, Fig.~\ref{fig:protocol}).

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{PEFT and adapter ensembling.} Bottleneck adapters \citep{houlsby2019parameter,pfeiffer2020adapterhub}, LoRA \citep{hu2021lora} and its variants \citep{dora,pissa,adalora,vera} make many adapters per task practical, and serving stacks share one base-model forward across thousands of them \citep{sheng2024slora,chen2024punica}. Combination strategies are \emph{prediction-space} (voting, logit or probability averaging \citep{lakshminarayanan2017simple}, including LoRA ensembles on LLMs \citep{wang2023loraensembles}, LoRA ensembles for uncertainty \citep{balabanov2024uncertainty,muhlematter2024loraensemble} and Bayesian LoRA \citep{yang2024bayesian}); \emph{weight-space} (soups \citep{wortsman2022model}, TIES \citep{yadav2023ties}, DARE \citep{yu2024dare}); and \emph{routing} \citep{pfeiffer2021adapterfusion,huang2023lorahub,adamix,mole,molora,hydralora,multilora}. We evaluate prediction-space rules (\texttt{soft\_vote}, \texttt{logit\_avg}, \texttt{majority\_vote}, and \texttt{greedy\_soup}, which greedily adds members on \texttt{val\_selection} and averages their logits); our weight-space merges are factor-wise variants (L16), and routing needs per-member logits that survive only for the runs of \S\ref{sec:a3} and twelve partial corpus pools (L15).

\paragraph{Pre-registration, variance and fair comparison.} Pre-registration is standard in clinical trials but rare in ML \citep{munafo2017manifesto,loken2017measurement} and has been proposed for NLP \citep{vanmiltenburg2021preregistering,bowman2021will}; fine-tuning seeds alone move results substantially \citep{dodge2020finetuning,bouthillier2021accounting}. Reported gains often shrink once baselines are tuned and budgets matched \citep{lucic2018gans,musgrave2020metric,dacrema2019progress,dodge2019show,reimers2017reporting}, efficiency claims need fixed compute \citep{dehghani2022efficiency}, and PEFT evaluation itself has been found unreliable \citep{chen2022revisiting}. \emph{An ensemble versus one bigger model at matched cost} is an open dispute: one wide network can match an ensemble of thin ones at a fixed budget \citep{chirkova2020deep}, the closest precedent for \S\ref{sec:a3}; a single larger network can replicate ensemble gains \citep{abe2022deep}, or not at matched FLOPs \citep{kondratyuk2020ensembling}; the optimum depends on budget \citep{lobacheva2020power}; ensembles over hyperparameters add diversity beyond seeds \citep{wenzel2020hyperparameter}; and the gain tracks the disagreement-to-error ratio \citep{theisen2023ensembles}. Our encoder result sides with \citeauthor{abe2022deep}, our decoder result the other way. What we add is the pre-registered, budget-matched test across families and the price of each protocol choice.

\paragraph{Calibration of fine-tuned LMs.} ECE was introduced by \citet{naeini2015} and popularised with equal-width bins by \citet{guo2017calibration}; we use 15 equal-mass bins and report Brier and NLL alongside, given ECE's estimator bias \citep{nixon2019} and refinements \citep{karandikar2021soft,minderer2021}. Pre-trained Transformers are reasonably calibrated in-domain and improved by temperature scaling \citep{desai2020calibration}; deep ensembles lower ECE \citep{lakshminarayanan2017simple}, but in-domain comparisons should be made after temperature scaling \citep{ashukha2020pitfalls}, and that scaling's gains erode under shift \citep{ovadia2019trust}. We apply the first point to PEFT, on both sides (\S\ref{sec:a3}).

\section{Benchmark composition}\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Pool types}

Per (model, task): \textbf{Pool-A} (seed-only), $20$ LoRA adapters at $r{=}8$, $\alpha{=}16$, lr $3{\times}10^{-4}$ on query/value, seeds varied; \textbf{Pool-B} (HP-diverse), $24$ adapters over $r{\in}\{4,8,16,32\}\times\alpha{\in}\{8,16,32\}\times$lr$\in\{1,3\}{\times}10^{-4}$; \textbf{Pool-C} (method-mixed), $\{$LoRA, DoRA, rsLoRA, PiSSA$\}\times5$ seeds.

\paragraph{Counting pools.} The release holds $99$ manifests: $73$ trained ensemble pools, $25$ \texttt{n\_rank} baseline pools and one smoke test. The pre-registered corpus is $67$ of the $73$ ($55$ v1.0 classification $+$ $12$ v1.1 HellaSwag/GSM8K); the other $6$ are v1.2 supplements (Table~\ref{tab:v11cells}). Where a table counts $80$ pools, it is the $55$ v1.0 pools plus the $25$ baselines, which also enter \texttt{best\_of\_n} comparisons. Realized sizes fall short of the design ($20$/$24$/$20$): median $19$, range $7$--$24$; baselines run $6$--$20$, so best-of-$N$ selection pressure varies by cell.

\paragraph{The \texttt{n\_rank} baseline, as run.} Per-pool grids are in App.~\ref{app:hp}. The pre-registration specified one adapter of rank $N\times$ the pool's; what was run is a search over $r{\in}\{16, 32\} \times \text{epochs}{\in}\{2, 4\} \times 5$ seeds, best on \texttt{val\_selection} (Table~\ref{tab:prexec}). Measured from the manifests it used a median $1.45\times$ the pool's training GPU-hours (max $2.96\times$). It is therefore a \emph{better-resourced} competitor that serves one adapter against the ensemble's $N$ --- neither budget-matched nor an upper bound on what a single adapter can reach. The equal-budget question is answered separately, by a pre-registered experiment (\S\ref{sec:a3}). \texttt{best\_of\_n} is the \texttt{val\_selection}-best member of the pool itself.

\subsection{Tasks and models}

The in-domain set is six classification tasks: MNLI (3-way NLI, $n_{\text{test}}{=}3{,}926$), QNLI (2{,}186), AG~News (4-way topic, 3{,}040), BoolQ (1{,}308), ANLI~R3 (adversarial NLI, 480), and SST-2 (350), all evaluated on the frozen 40\% \texttt{test} slice of the split described above. ANLI, SST-2 and BoolQ fall below $n_{\text{test}}{=}2{,}000$ and are the source of the low-precision cells quantified in \S\ref{sec:findings}; dropping small tasks post hoc is itself a shortcut, so we keep them and stratify by test-set size wherever the null is at stake. ANLI also serves as an OOD target for MNLI-trained pools (App.~\ref{app:ood}), on disjoint pools, never pooled into one cell.

Architectures span two families. \emph{Encoders} (sequence classification): BERT-base, BERT-large, RoBERTa-base, DeBERTa-v3-base. \emph{Decoders} (causal LM with a classification head or exact-match decoding): Qwen2.5-\{0.5B, 1.5B, 3B, 7B\}, TinyLlama-1.1B, Pythia-1.4B, SmolLM2-1.7B, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, and Mistral-7B. Encoder and decoder cells behave differently enough (App.~\ref{app:landscape}) that we never pool them; \S\ref{sec:findings} reports the two families side by side.

\paragraph{v1.1 extensions.} v1.1 adds multi-choice (HellaSwag) and generation (GSM8K) through twelve further pools, bringing the pre-registered corpus to \textbf{67} pools; both families are excluded from every headline statistic (L9, L12; App.~\ref{app:v11}).

\subsection{Adjudication protocol}

Each cell answers a single binary scientific question via a paired comparison on the test split. We use the same 40/20/40 \texttt{val\_selection}/\texttt{val\_combine}/\texttt{test} split (seed 0) for every cell. For each (pool $\times$ method $\times$ baseline\_kind $\times$ metric):
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Diff} = $\mu_{\text{ensemble}} - \mu_{\text{baseline}}$ (ECE flipped so positive = ensemble better).
\item \textbf{Bootstrap CI} \citep{efron1979bootstrap}: percentile, $B{=}5000$, paired over examples. For the descriptive cross-pool frontier fit, a cluster bootstrap over pools (App.~\ref{app:stats}).
\item \textbf{$p$-values} (two-sided): accuracy, the exact null of the paired sign-flip test, i.e.\ a binomial test on the discordant examples; ECE, inverted paired bootstrap ($B{=}5000$, capped at $1$), as a sign-flip test does not apply to ECE (L14).
\item \textbf{Within-table Holm correction} \citep{holm1979simple}: applied to every multi-cell table; significance markers ($^{*}$) reflect Holm-adjusted $p$.
\item \textbf{Benjamini--Hochberg FDR} \citep{bh1995} per arm at $q{=}0.05$ over the $927$ valid cells ($515$ accuracy, $412$ ECE; the $100$ HellaSwag cells and one smoke-test cell enter no batch): a cell keeps its CI verdict only if it passes, else it is \emph{unsupported}. Counts are post-FDR unless marked pre (App.~\ref{app:stats}).
\item \textbf{Adjudication label}: \emph{supported} if CI lower bound $> 0$; \emph{reversed} if upper bound $< 0$; \emph{unsupported} otherwise.
\end{itemize}

\section{The adjudicated landscape}
\label{sec:findings}

\paragraph{Against the better-resourced single.} Against \texttt{n\_rank} the clean encoder slice contains \textbf{no} cell with a significant accuracy gain ($0$ of $92$, before and after FDR) and $27$ significant reversals ($29\%$; mean $\Delta{=}{-}1.27$pp). On the six pools closest to budget parity ($0.90$--$1.25\times$) it is $0/24$ SUPPORTED and $8$ REVERSED, and pool-level $\Delta$ is not significantly related to the budget ratio (Spearman $\rho{=}{-}0.28$, $p{=}0.20$) --- which does not show the budget is irrelevant; \S\ref{sec:a3} tests that directly. On the $25$ decoder pools the same comparison gives $13$ of $100$ cells SUPPORTED ($31$ pre-FDR), none REVERSED, mean ${+}0.56$pp, all on MNLI in $5$ pools (\texttt{family\_\allowbreak split.\allowbreak py}). Dropping every comparison whose arms mostly failed to train (final loss within $15\%$ of a uniform guess's; test-free) leaves decoders at $9/96$ and encoders at $0/72$ with $29\%$ REVERSED (\texttt{training\_\allowbreak health.\allowbreak py}). On the \emph{same} $92$ encoder cells ECE improves in $51$ and worsens in $17$ ($57$ and $10$ of $100$ for decoders) --- against a single that was never calibrated (\S\ref{sec:a3}).

\paragraph{How many independent observations this is.} The cells are not independent: four rules share each pool, and pools of the same (model, task) share one baseline and one test set. The $23$ encoder pools form $10$ baseline clusters and the $25$ decoder pools $15$. At that level (\texttt{dependence\_\allowbreak robustness.\allowbreak py}) the encoder mean is ${-}1.20$pp (cluster bootstrap $[{-}2.30, {-}0.37]$) but only $8$ of $10$ clusters are negative (sign test $p{=}0.11$); the decoder mean is ${+}0.66$pp $[{-}0.09, {+}1.37]$ with $14$ of $15$ clusters positive ($p{=}0.001$). Benjamini--Yekutieli, valid under arbitrary dependence, gives $0$ SUPPORTED and $17$ REVERSED encoder cells and $8$ SUPPORTED decoder cells. The corpus therefore establishes the direction of each family's effect only weakly; the pre-registered experiment of \S\ref{sec:a3}, with independent seeds and sealed test sets, is the stronger evidence. \emph{Precision.} We report which gains the CIs exclude rather than counting silence as evidence --- exclusion of a $+1$pp gain, not two-sided equivalence, and the $1$pp threshold was not pre-registered: $67/92$ encoder cells exclude it ($62$ of the $68$ with $n_{\text{test}}{\geq}2000$, median CI half-width $0.91$pp; the $24$ small-$n$ cells, $3.85$pp, are uninformative). \emph{Procedure dependence.} No procedure finds an encoder accuracy gain; the REVERSED count does depend on the procedure ($38$ raw, $15$ within-table Holm, $27$ BH, $12$ Holm over the whole accuracy arm), and the ECE count much less ($52/43/51$; Holm over the ECE arm is unreachable at the bootstrap's $1/5001$ resolution; App.~\ref{app:correction}).

\paragraph{The corpus as a whole, and why we headline a slice of it.} Over the $927$ valid cells the picture is mixed, not negative: $34.5\%$ SUPPORTED and $14.9\%$ REVERSED pre-correction, $27.8\%$ and $13.2\%$ after per-arm BH. Most of it is against the pool's own best member --- the weak baseline of \S\ref{sec:protocol} --- so we lead instead with the $92$ encoder and $100$ decoder classification cells against \texttt{n\_rank}, and report both families rather than the half that tells the cleaner story.

\paragraph{Where the deltas sit.} \textbf{(P$_a$)} Against the pool's own best member (\texttt{best\_of\_n}) encoder ensembles win in $9.8\%$ of the $92$ matched cells and lose in $7.6\%$; against the better-resourced \texttt{n\_rank} single the same pools win in none --- shortcut S2 (\S\ref{sec:protocol}) measured on our own corpus. Two families are excluded from every headline statistic and every multiplicity batch: \textbf{(P$_b$)} the HellaSwag pools, whose evaluator is not consistent across runs (L9, App.~\ref{app:hellaswag}); and \textbf{(P$_c$)} the decoder GSM8K pools, whose \texttt{best\_single} was selected on the split it is evaluated on (App.~\ref{app:gsm8k-scaling}, L12).

\paragraph{What the ECE count is measured against.} The $51$ is measured against a single selected by validation \emph{accuracy} and never calibrated --- on its own candidate set it sits at a median $90$th percentile of test ECE. On the six encoder corpus pools whose member logits survive (against \texttt{best\_of\_n}), one temperature fitted on \texttt{val\_combine} moves the ensemble from better-calibrated in $4/6$ pools to $2/6$, but the per-pool differences after scaling both arms all have ECE CIs spanning $0$, and NLL still favours the ensemble in all six ($3$ significantly); without ANLI the ECE gain against the scaled single is ${+}0.0004$. Six decoder pools rebuilt later from surviving adapters (exploratory) show the same NLL pattern more strongly ($6/6$ significant) (App.~\ref{app:tempmech}). The corpus therefore cannot say whether the population buys reliability a scalar cannot. The pre-registered experiment of \S\ref{sec:a3} can, because it keeps every member's logits.

\paragraph{Two qualifications on the $51$.} It is an ECE result, not a proper-score one --- the multiclass Brier improves in only $34$ of the same $92$ cells --- and it belongs to probability averaging: \texttt{majority\_vote} supplies $16$ of the $17$ reversals, and its vote-fraction ``confidence'' is not a probability estimate (App.~\ref{app:ecequal}).


\paragraph{What predicts the deltas (descriptive).} A diversity--quality regression fitted across pools tracks the accuracy deltas (LOPO-CV $R^2{=}0.60$ over $48$ pools, though its cluster-bootstrap CI spans zero), but its prediction-space features were computed partly on the test examples and include a label-dependent error-overlap term (L1c), so we report it as a descriptive correlation, not a predictor (App.~\ref{app:scatter}). Three candidate mechanisms for the family split fail their tests (App.~\ref{app:mech}).

\section{A pre-registered equal-budget test}\label{sec:a3}

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.86\textwidth]{figures/fig_a3_equal_budget.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{Equal budget, sealed test sets} (\S\ref{sec:a3}). Ensemble pipeline minus single pipeline, at budgets of $4$, $8$ and $16$ epochs over $20$k training rows (equal tokens on both sides). Top: accuracy; bottom: NLL after one temperature per arm, fitted on \texttt{val\_combine} (lower is better for the ensemble). Mean of two seed-disjoint replicates; bars are $95\%$ paired bootstrap CIs over test examples ($9{,}824$ SNLI, $60{,}000$ Yahoo Answers). Dark solid lines: the step~9.1 pair; light dashed lines: the size-matched step~9.4 pair. The one off-scale cell is a single failed run, the only single candidate of one replicate at $B{=}4$.}
\label{fig:a3}
\end{figure*}

\S\ref{sec:findings} compares a pool with a \emph{better-resourced} single, so it cannot say how one budget should be spent, and the corpus kept no member logits with which to calibrate both sides. We therefore ran a new experiment whose design, budgets, second task, predictions and pass rules were written down before any of its runs (registration step~9.1 of the released file \texttt{mechanism/\allowbreak PREREG\_AB.md}; later steps 9.2--9.7 add to it, each before its own runs), with the analysis code committed before the test predictions were unsealed.

\paragraph{Design.} Two backbones (BERT-base; Qwen2.5-0.5B), two tasks --- SNLI and Yahoo Answers topics, neither in the corpus --- and three budgets $B{\in}\{4,8,16\}$ epochs over $20$k training rows. The ensemble pipeline $E(B)$ trains $B/2$ rank-$8$ adapters for $2$ epochs and averages their probabilities; the single pipeline $S(B)$ trains $B/4$ rank-$32$ adapters for $4$ epochs and keeps the \texttt{val\_selection}-best, so every search run is charged to it. Both process exactly the same training tokens, and LoRA rank changes training FLOPs by well under $1\%$. Two replicates use disjoint seeds; no run failed to train, and one run killed by an unrelated job on the shared GPU was rerun with the same seed. The predictions were not blind to the backbones: an exploratory pilot on the SNLI \emph{validation} split (the H8 runs and a $12$-run rank$\times$epoch pilot) preceded them, which is why the test splits were sealed and Yahoo Answers was added. The registered predictions: at $B{\in}\{8,16\}$, on both tasks, the ensemble is worse than the single on BERT and better on Qwen, in accuracy and in NLL after one temperature per arm; each prediction replicates if none of its $8$ cells is REVERSED and at least $4$ are SUPPORTED.

\paragraph{Results.} Both predictions replicate, $8/8$ cells each, and the four unpredicted $B{=}4$ cells agree (Fig.~\ref{fig:a3}). At $B{=}16$ the ensemble loses $2.8$pp on SNLI and $1.0$pp on Yahoo with BERT and gains $1.2$ and $3.2$pp with Qwen; after temperature scaling on both sides its NLL is higher by $0.063$ and $0.025$ (BERT) and lower by $0.059$ and $0.125$ (Qwen). Risk at $90\%$ coverage moves the same way, and both replicates agree in sign in every cell. The Qwen single is badly overconfident (fitted $T$ $2.0$--$2.9$, against $1.0$--$1.1$ for the ensemble): scaling removes most of its NLL deficit, not all of it. On these two backbones, then, an equal budget is better spent on one adapter for the encoder and on a pool for the decoder, and the decoder pool buys reliability that one temperature does not.

\paragraph{A single that tunes its recipe.} $S$ uses one recipe (rank $32$, lr $3{\times}10^{-4}$), which might simply be mistuned for Qwen. A second registered step (step~9.2 of the same file, written after the results above and committed before its runs) lets the single search rank $\{8,32\}\times$ learning rate $\{10^{-4},3{\times}10^{-4}\}$, one $4$-epoch run each, inside the same $B{=}16$. Both predictions replicate again, $4/4$ cells each. On BERT the search keeps the original recipe and nothing changes ($-2.6$ and $-1.1$pp). On Qwen it narrows the gap without closing it: $+1.1$pp on SNLI and $+1.7$pp on Yahoo (from $+3.2$), with calibrated NLL still lower by $0.048$ and $0.067$. None of the four Qwen configurations reaches the ensemble even when picked on the test set ($87.3$ vs $88.4\%$ on SNLI, $73.8$ vs $75.6\%$ on Yahoo, replicate means).

\paragraph{An ensemble that tunes too.} A fourth registered step (step~9.6) gives the ensemble the same freedom: eight $2$-epoch members, two seeds for each of rank $\{8,32\}\times$ learning rate $\{10^{-4},3{\times}10^{-4}\}$, combined by greedy selection on \texttt{val\_selection}, against the tuned single at the same $B{=}16$. Both predictions replicate ($4/4$ cells each), but the encoder loss shrinks to $-0.9$pp on SNLI and $-0.5$pp on Yahoo, while the decoder gain is $+0.8$ and $+1.5$pp.

\paragraph{Where the gap comes from (post hoc).} Ensemble and single still differ in averaging, member recipe and selection at once. Two analyses of the already-unsealed test logits separate them (exploratory; \texttt{a3\_\allowbreak decompose.\allowbreak py}, \texttt{a3\_\allowbreak samebank.\allowbreak py}). Splitting $\Delta$ into an averaging term (the ensemble minus its own \texttt{val\_selection}-best member) and a recipe term (that member minus the single), averaging adds $+0.4$ to $+0.8$pp in all $8$ backbone$\times$task cells, encoders included, and the family split lies entirely in the recipe term ($-1.4$ to $-3.3$pp on encoders, $-0.1$ to $+2.4$pp on decoders). Built from the \emph{same} eight runs, the tuned ensemble beats the best single run of that bank in all four cells ($+0.1$ to $+0.5$pp, calibrated NLL lower), but against a single that may spend the budget on $4$-epoch, rank-$32$ runs with early stopping the encoder still loses ($-0.9$, $-0.2$pp) and the decoder wins ($+0.8$, $+1.1$pp). Averaging, then, helps both families a little; what flips by family is whether the budget buys a single adapter a better recipe --- the quality-gap term that also separates the families in the corpus. A registered test of both effects on six new replicates, with a replicate-level interval as primary (step~9.7), had not run at the time of writing (L18).

\paragraph{Family or size?} BERT-base ($110$M) and Qwen-0.5B ($494$M) differ in size as well as family, so a third registered step (step~9.4, before its runs) repeats the design on a size-matched pair from opposite families: BERT-large ($335$M, encoder) and SmolLM2-360M ($362$M, decoder). Both predictions replicate: of the $8$ predicted accuracy cells $6$ are SUPPORTED and none REVERSED, and $8/8$ calibrated-NLL cells. At matched size the encoder ensemble loses ($-0.9$ to $-2.0$pp) and the decoder ensemble wins on Yahoo ($+1.1$pp) and ties on SNLI ($+0.3$pp, not significant); after temperature scaling its NLL is lower on both. Two BERT-large runs failed to train and stay in their pipelines as registered; one was the only single candidate of a replicate at $B{=}4$, which produces the one off-scale, unpredicted cell in Fig.~\ref{fig:a3}.

\paragraph{What this does and does not identify.} Across four backbones the sign of the equal-budget comparison follows the family, not the size, but four backbones from two families cannot say which property of a decoder causes it. The experiments cover classification, budgets up to $16$ epoch-units ($8$ members), and one temperature per arm as the post-hoc competitor.

The corpus lets us ask a question the ensembling literature has not asked directly: \emph{how much of the reported benefit is produced by the evaluation protocol rather than by ensembling?} We isolate three evaluation choices (Table~\ref{tab:protocol}), each open to any study of this kind, and measure what each is worth on our corpus (we do not measure how often published work makes them); all three are recomputed from released artifacts alone. A shortcut here is not a mistake that always flatters ensembling --- it is a degree of freedom that flatters whichever arm is given it. We report each one's size and say which arm it favours.

\paragraph{S1: selecting the single adapter on test.} A pool's ``best single'' is often reported at its test-set maximum. Reconstructing that oracle from \texttt{individual\_\allowbreak accuracy\_\allowbreak test} and comparing against the held-out \texttt{val\_selection} choice, test-selection is worth $+1.12$pp on average ($+1.30$pp on encoders; median $+0.57$, max $+9.58$) and inflates $82\%$ of pools. This is a free gain awarded to whichever arm is allowed to peek. In our corpus the \emph{single} peeks, so S1 makes the ensemble look \emph{worse}; given to the ensemble's member selection, as is more common, it works the other way.

\paragraph{S2: comparing against a weak single.} Ensembles are often compared with the pool's own best member rather than with a single given at least the pool's budget; our pre-registration fixed both comparisons. The two baselines do not cover identical pool sets ($80$ pools have \texttt{best\_of\_n}, $48$ have \texttt{n\_rank}), so we compare them on the $48$ that carry both, where every cell has a twin under the other baseline (Fig.~\ref{fig:protocol}A). Against the pool's best member, $21.9\%$ of cells are SUPPORTED and $3.6\%$ REVERSED; against the stronger \texttt{n\_rank} single (\S\ref{sec:findings}: median $1.45\times$ the pool's compute), SUPPORTED falls to $6.8\%$ and REVERSED rises to $14.1\%$. On the clean encoder slice the effect is total: \textbf{$0$ of $92$ cells are SUPPORTED} (identically pre- and post-FDR) while $29.3\%$ are REVERSED, with mean $\Delta=-1.27$pp (Fig.~\ref{fig:protocol}B). The result holds for all four combination rules --- \texttt{greedy\_soup}, \texttt{soft\_vote}, \texttt{logit\_avg}, \texttt{majority\_vote} at $0\%$ SUPPORTED, REVERSED $21.7/26.1/26.1/43.5\%$ --- though \texttt{soft\_vote} and \texttt{logit\_avg} agree to within $0.1$pp in $20$ of $23$ pools, so this is roughly three distinct rules. On decoders the same shortcut moves SUPPORTED from $33\%$ to $13\%$.

\paragraph{S3: reporting task means.} Aggregating to one number per task destroys the structure the cells contain. MNLI has a task-level mean of $-0.012$pp --- it reads as ``ensembling makes no difference here'' --- yet that average conceals $30$ statistically significant cells ($17$ REVERSED, $13$ SUPPORTED) pointing in opposite directions --- the reversals are encoder cells and the gains decoder cells, so what the task mean hides here is the family split itself. Across the corpus, four tasks whose means read as no-effect ($|\overline{\Delta}|<0.5$pp) together hide $36$ significant cells, $22\%$ of their cells.

\section{Practical implications}\label{sec:distill}\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What the decoder gain costs.} At an equal \emph{training} budget the pool is not free at inference: measured on one GPU at batch $32$, an $8$-member ensemble takes $11$--$13\times$ the latency of a merged single on Qwen-0.5B and $14$--$18\times$ on BERT-base, depending on whether members run in sequence or in one mixed-adapter pass (App.~\ref{app:latency}); a dedicated multi-LoRA kernel would narrow that (L8). \emph{Distillation} \citep{hinton2015distilling}, the standard way to remove that cost, recovers the ensemble's accuracy in only $1$ of $4$ corpus cells (App.~\ref{app:distill}).

\paragraph{Scope.} The equal-budget result covers four backbones from two families and two classification tasks; it shows that the sign of the comparison follows the family at matched training cost and matched size, not which property of the backbone sets it. The audit covers $N{\leq}24$, rank $16$--$32$ and $2$--$4$ epochs, and its \texttt{n\_steps} and \texttt{n\_data} baselines were filled in only $3$ and $0$ cells. Much larger pools, members more diverse than seed, hyperparameters and PEFT method, and generation tasks are outside what we measured.

\paragraph{Takeaway.} Measure against a single adapter given the same budget and the same post-hoc calibration, on your backbone. Where we did: on BERT-base and BERT-large the budget is better spent on one adapter, which also calibrates better after one temperature; on Qwen2.5-0.5B and SmolLM2-360M (decoders of at most $0.5$B; larger ones untested) a pool of rank-$8$ adapters buys up to $3$pp of accuracy and a lower calibrated NLL at the same training cost, paid for at serving time. The released subset and adjudication code let that comparison be repeated for a new rule or backbone without retraining our pools.

\section*{Limitations}

\paragraph{What this paper does \emph{not} claim.} App.~\ref{app:delta} sets what is new here against prior PEFT-ensembling work, cell by cell. For reviewer transparency, we enumerate the inferences that our 1{,}028 cells do \emph{not} support: \textbf{(a)} we do \emph{not} claim a single best combination method --- against \texttt{n\_rank} on clean encoder cells every rule loses on average ($-0.9$ to $-1.7$pp, \texttt{greedy\_soup} least), and \texttt{majority\_vote} supplies most calibration reversals; the apparent \texttt{majority\_vote} advantage on $\geq 1.5$B decoders rests on GSM8K and does not survive decontamination (L12), so we withdraw it; \textbf{(b)} we do \emph{not} claim ensembling is universally beneficial --- $14.9\%$ of the $927$ valid cells are REVERSED pre-FDR ($13.2\%$ post-FDR), and $27$ of $92$ encoder accuracy cells are reversed against \texttt{n\_rank}, while \S\ref{sec:a3} shows the decoder pool winning at equal budget; \textbf{(c)} we do \emph{not} claim our diversity-quality frontier transfers to other PEFT families (LoHa, AdaLoRA, prompt tuning) or to instruction-tuned base models; \textbf{(d)} we do \emph{not} claim distillation generalizes --- accuracy recovery: Qwen-0.5B $87\%$, Qwen-1.5B $4.5\%$, BERT-base fails, BERT-large $-181\%$ ($1$-of-$4$ cells positive); \textbf{(e)} we do \emph{not} claim the decoders' lower $H(c\,|\,\mathrm{conf})$ reflects sharper confidence: it is almost entirely their higher accuracy. Each of these is a deliberate scope-limit, not a hedge: extending any of them is a defined v1.2 cell that can be added without retraining the existing 67 pools.

\paragraph{L1 — Scope of the null result.} Our headline finding --- no significant \emph{accuracy} gain against a stronger single adapter --- is established on encoder classification cells, against the \texttt{n\_rank} baseline, at our pool sizes ($N{\leq}24$) and adapter budgets (rank $16$--$32$, $2$--$4$ epochs), on $92$ cells from $23$ pools. It is \emph{not} a claim that ensembling never helps: on decoder classification it does, modestly ($13/100$ cells, \S\ref{sec:findings}), and the null does not cover generation, larger pools, non-classification output spaces, or budgets where a stronger single adapter is not trainable. \texttt{n\_rank} spends the pool's budget on rank; other allocations (more steps, more data) are separate baselines in the corpus and are not the headline. We report all cells rather than filter to the favourable ones.

\paragraph{L1b — HellaSwag cells are excluded from the strict analysis.} A second, independent anomaly on the same family --- a two-regime collapse under shared hyperparameters --- is documented in App.~\ref{app:tworegime}. The $100$ v1.1 multichoice cells were produced by a version of our evaluator that performed selection on \texttt{val\_combine} rather than the held-out \texttt{val\_selection} split, which optimistically biases selection-dependent methods (\texttt{best\_single}, \texttt{greedy\_soup}) and the LoRAHub-style continuous-weight baseline that optimises on that same split. We therefore exclude HellaSwag from \S\ref{sec:protocol} and mark its findings provisional (P$_b$, App.~\ref{app:lorahub}) pending a corrected rerun, which requires re-evaluating those pools and is not included here. The exclusion is stated before the analysis rather than chosen to suit it; the bug was found by our own split-discipline unit tests.

\paragraph{L1c — The frontier's features touch the test set.} \texttt{diversity\_\allowbreak metrics.\allowbreak py} loads each adapter's cached logits without split indexing, so the prediction-space features include the test examples the target $\Delta$ is measured on, and the error-overlap feature also uses their labels. On the pools whose cache survives, restricting disagreement, logit correlation and entropy to \texttt{val\_selection} moves them by $0.1$--$2.8\%$ and preserves the pool ordering (\texttt{leakage\_\allowbreak check\_\allowbreak diversity.\allowbreak py}), but that check does not cover the label-dependent feature, and a leak-free re-fit needs member logits that were not retained. The frontier is therefore descriptive only (App.~\ref{app:scatter}).

\paragraph{L1d — The headline cells are not independent tests.} The $92$ encoder cells are $23$ pools $\times$ $4$ rules, two of them near-duplicates ($20/23$ pools within $0.1$pp), and pools of one (model, task) share a baseline and a test set: $10$ independent clusters for encoders, $15$ for decoders. BH assumes independence or positive dependence. We therefore also report cluster-level sign tests and bootstraps and Benjamini--Yekutieli counts (\S\ref{sec:findings}, \texttt{dependence\_\allowbreak robustness.\allowbreak py}). At the cluster level the encoder sign test is not significant ($p{=}0.11$) and the decoder CI on the mean includes $0$; the corpus fixes the direction of each family's effect only weakly, which is why \S\ref{sec:a3} exists.

\paragraph{L2 — Decoder cross-pool predictability fails at our scale.} The fitted relation is plotted per pool in App.~\ref{app:scatter}. 25 decoder pools is too little variation across pools to extract a stable cross-pool LOPO regression signal for accuracy gain (corpus-wide accuracy fit $R^2{=}0.60$, encoder-only $0.62$, decoder $R^2{=}{-}1.14$, Table~\ref{tab:frontier_strat}; descriptive, L1c). Larger decoder pool counts may close this gap; we leave it open.

\paragraph{L3 — \texttt{greedy\_soup} is close to a random subset on seed-only pools.} On converged seed-only Pool-A cells, \texttt{greedy\_soup} is often statistically indistinguishable from a random subset of the same size; its larger gains appeared only on the excluded HellaSwag pools (L9), from which we draw no conclusion.

\paragraph{L4 — OOD accuracy rarely transfers; diversity \emph{hurts} OOD.} ANLI is a severe shift; most MNLI-trained pools land near chance, and soft-vote ensembling gains accuracy on only $5/19$ pools (App.~\ref{app:ood}). The Pool-B (HP-diverse) and Pool-C (method-mixed) configurations that improve in-distribution ensembling \emph{consistently hurt} OOD accuracy --- the diversity our framework optimizes for is in-distribution disagreement, not robustness. v1.1 OOD evaluation (HellaSwag$\to$ARC, GSM8K$\to$MATH) is left to v1.2.

\paragraph{L5 — GSM8K ECE methodology is non-standard.} We report 15-bin equal-mass ECE on per-example confidence $=\!\exp(\text{mean per-token logprob})$ over the model's generation, with ensemble confidence aggregating only over adapters voting for the majority answer. Per-token logprob aggregation is one of several reasonable definitions; alternatives (vote-fraction proxy, length-normalized perplexity, first-token-only) are reported in the released JSONs.

\paragraph{L6 — Pool-B/C HellaSwag adapter quality is uneven.} On RoBERTa and DeBERTa, $\geq 14$ of $20$--$22$ Pool-B HellaSwag adapters fail to converge above chance under our shared HP grid. We report these pools rather than filter them, but the HellaSwag cells are excluded from every statistic (L9), so their quality affects no claim.

\paragraph{L7 — Shared HP grid is deliberate; we ran HP-tuned re-evaluations as v1.2 cells.} Pool-A uses a single $(r{=}8, \alpha{=}16, lr{=}3{\times}10^{-4})$ HP across all architectures. We added two v1.2 cells testing the HP-confound hypothesis from \emph{both} directions: \textbf{(a)} an architecture-tuned re-run of Pool-A RoBERTa HellaSwag at $lr{=}2{\times}10^{-5}$ (per \citealp{liu2019roberta}) eliminates the under-convergence completely --- all $10/10$ tuned-seed adapters converge to val\_acc $\in [0.478, 0.505]$, mean $0.491$ (vs.\ the original shared-HP $14/20$ below chance, median $0.164$); \textbf{(b)} the symmetric check, BERT-base HellaSwag at $lr{=}2{\times}10^{-5}$ (RoBERTa's natural lr applied to BERT), \emph{does not collapse}: $8/8$ seed re-runs converge to mean $0.329 \pm 0.011$ (range $[0.317, 0.345]$), only $\sim 3$pp below BERT-base at its default $lr{=}3{\times}10^{-4}$ ($0.358$) and far above the $0.097$ collapse RoBERTa exhibits under the same $lr{=}3{\times}10^{-4}$ shared HP. These are training-time validation accuracies of the members, consistent with RoBERTa being sensitive to the shared learning rate; because the multichoice ensemble evaluator is not consistent across runs (L9), we draw no conclusion about ensembling from these pools. The shared-HP cells are retained in v1.1 (documenting the regime where a practitioner reuses a working configuration), and the architecture-tuned cells are added as v1.2 (\S\ref{app:prereg}).

\paragraph{L8 — Serving cost is measured on one GPU, at one batch size.} Our earlier vLLM numbers ($1.07\times$ at $N{=}8$) timed requests each routed to \emph{one} of $8$ adapters, which is multi-tenant overhead, not ensembling; they are no longer cited as ensemble cost. The ensemble cost we now report (App.~\ref{app:latency}) is measured directly but on one shared A6000, at batch $32$, with PEFT's generic kernels; a dedicated multi-LoRA kernel \citep{chen2024punica,sheng2024slora} would narrow the gap to the merged single, and we did not measure tail latency under production load.

\paragraph{L9 — The multichoice evaluator is not consistent across runs.} For one HellaSwag adapter, accuracy on $5{,}000$-example subsets ranges over $\pm 10$pp and one subset implies more correct answers than the full validation set contains (App.~\ref{app:splitvar}); no single prediction vector produces both, so the evaluation path changed between runs, for a reason we have not found. Earlier versions called this subsample variance, which it cannot be. The v1.1 multichoice cells (Table~\ref{tab:v11cells}) are therefore uninterpretable, not merely subset-conditional; they were already outside every headline statistic (L1b).

\paragraph{L10 — DeBERTa-v3 HellaSwag HP search did not recover.} Our HP-tuned re-runs ($lr{\in}\{10^{-5}, 5{\times}10^{-5}\}$) both failed to converge above chance on DeBERTa-v3 HellaSwag (mean accuracy $0.252$, range $[0.247, 0.257]$ across 10 tuned-seed adapters), in contrast to RoBERTa where $lr{=}2{\times}10^{-5}$ fixes convergence. This suggests the DeBERTa-v3 + multichoice-LoRA combination has a deeper architecture-LoRA interaction issue (possibly with the disentangled attention) that simple HP tuning does not address. A wider DeBERTa HP/target-module sweep is a defined v1.3 cell.

\paragraph{L11 — D1--D5 are post-hoc heuristics, not pre-registered.} The decision rules (App.~\ref{app:rules}) were distilled from inspection of the $1{,}028$-cell corpus and are not part of the Phase-0 pre-registration. The Table~\ref{tab:cost} cost-savings comparison uses the same corpus that the rules were derived from, so it is an \emph{upper bound on rule-based routing}, not a held-out generalization estimate. Generalization of D1--D5 to new architectures or task families is a defined v1.3 evaluation cell.

\paragraph{L12 — GSM8K cells carry no defensible positive claim, and our contamination probe was invalid.} Two separate issues affect the GSM8K cells, and we distinguish them. \textbf{(i) Selection on test (valid, disqualifying).} \texttt{ensemble\_\allowbreak eval\_\allowbreak gsm8k.\allowbreak py} selects \texttt{best\_single} by per-adapter accuracy on the same test split it is then evaluated on. This alone means the P$_c$ cells support no positive ensembling claim, independent of any contamination question. \textbf{(ii) Contamination (withdrawn).} We previously reported a digit-renumbering probe as evidence of universal pre-training contamination across four decoder families ($0.45$--$0.66 \to 0.02$--$0.06$). \emph{That probe was invalid and we withdraw the finding.} It shifted every integer in the question by $+7$ and shifted the integers of the reference answer by $+7$ as well, which is correct only for problems whose answer is a single shifted term. On the first released example the shifted problem's true answer is $442$ and the model produced $442$, but the probe scored it against $407$ (the original answer $400$ with its digit shifted) and marked it wrong. Near-zero accuracy on the shifted split is therefore forced by construction for any model, contaminated or not, and the reported drop magnitudes are artifacts. Note that their size should have been a warning: template-perturbation studies report gaps of roughly $10$--$13$pp \citep{mirzadeh2024gsmsymbolic, zhang2024gsm1k}, not $43$--$62$pp. Whether these cells are contaminated is now an \emph{open question}; answering it requires template regeneration with recomputed gold answers in the style of those works. We report this failure rather than silently dropping the probe, because a paper about protocol-induced conclusions should hold its own probes to the same standard.

\paragraph{L13 — The corpus has no calibration competitor at scale.} The single behind the corpus's ECE counts is selected by validation accuracy and never calibrated, and member logits survive for only six encoder pools (six decoder pools were later rebuilt from surviving adapters, with $6$--$10$ of their members). There, both arms temperature-scaled, no pool's ECE difference is significant, NLL still favours the ensemble in all six, and the earlier claim that one temperature ``erases'' the advantage rested largely on ANLI; we withdraw it. The competitor is supplied in \S\ref{sec:a3}, where both arms are scaled on every pool. Temperature scaling's gains erode under shift where ensembles' may not \citep{ovadia2019trust}; we did not test OOD.

\paragraph{L14 — Our earlier ECE $p$-values tested the wrong null (corrected here).} \texttt{compute\_\allowbreak match.\_\allowbreak sign\_\allowbreak flip\_\allowbreak pvalue\_\allowbreak ece} permutes the (confidence, correctness) \emph{pair} between arms, which tests whether the two systems share one joint distribution --- something averaging violates by construction whether or not ECE moves (median $p$ $4\times10^{-4}$ in the ECE arm against $0.13$ for accuracy). Earlier versions fed those $p$-values into one BH batch over all $1{,}028$ cells, which also loosened the accuracy cutoff from $0.0145$ to $0.0248$. Every ECE cell now carries a valid $p$ from the inverted paired bootstrap and BH runs per arm (\texttt{scripts/\allowbreak apply\_\allowbreak bh\_\allowbreak by\_\allowbreak arm.\allowbreak py}; the old fields are kept under \texttt{legacy\_\allowbreak mixed\_\allowbreak batch}). The correction lowered decoder accuracy SUPPORTED from $19$ to $14$, encoder REVERSED from $31$ to $29$ and encoder ECE SUPPORTED from $52$ to $51$. A second correction (\texttt{scripts/\allowbreak apply\_\allowbreak bh\_\allowbreak valid\_\allowbreak exact.\allowbreak py}) removed the $101$ invalid cells (HellaSwag, smoke test) from the BH batches, replaced the Monte Carlo accuracy $p$ by the sign-flip test's exact binomial null, and capped $7$ ECE $p$-values that had reached $2$; it moved decoder SUPPORTED to $13$ and encoder REVERSED to $27$ ($6$ verdicts changed). The encoder accuracy null is unchanged throughout.

\paragraph{L15 — What the release can and cannot do.} \emph{Re-adjudication}: every corpus cell can be re-tested from its released per-example predictions and confidences (accuracy and ECE). \emph{New combination rules}: only on pools whose per-member logits survive --- the $264$ runs of \S\ref{sec:a3} with its pilot and checks (validation and test logits in the anonymous release; adapter weights released at de-anonymisation) and twelve partial corpus pools ($6$--$10$ surviving members each, against \texttt{best\_of\_n}); the corpus's $23$ headline pools kept none, so a new rule cannot be scored on them without retraining. \emph{Re-training}: adapter weights exist for $253$ of $1{,}744$ corpus adapters, and $1{,}027$ of $1{,}028$ result JSONs record \texttt{git\_dirty}, so byte-level replay is unavailable.

\paragraph{L16 — Our weight-space merges operate on LoRA factors, not on $\Delta W$.} \texttt{weight\_\allowbreak space\_\allowbreak merge.\allowbreak py} averages the \texttt{lora\_A} and \texttt{lora\_B} tensors independently, and $\mathrm{mean}(B)\,\mathrm{mean}(A) \neq \mathrm{mean}(BA)$, so the uniform/TIES/DARE numbers in App.~\ref{app:repro} are a factor-wise variant rather than the published methods. The prediction-space \texttt{uniform\_soup} proxy is unaffected (it is marked as a proxy).

\paragraph{L17 — Generation-family adapters were trained without label masking.} \texttt{real\_\allowbreak lora\_\allowbreak train.\allowbreak py} pads to \texttt{max\_length} and copies \texttt{input\_ids} into \texttt{labels}, so padding and prompt tokens contribute to the causal-LM loss. This affects the GSM8K pools only; the encoder classification pools and the multiple-choice trainer mask correctly.

\paragraph{L18 — One registered test had not run.} Registration step 9.7 tests the averaging and allocation effects of \S\ref{sec:a3} on six new replicates with replicate-level intervals. It was registered, with its analysis code, before any of its runs, but the shared GPUs were occupied by another user's job and it had not run at the time of writing. The averaging/allocation split in \S\ref{sec:a3} therefore rests on post-hoc analysis of already-unsealed test sets.

\paragraph{Reproducibility.} App.~\ref{app:prexec} sets what was pre-registered against what was run. Every released JSON carries the provenance block whose schema is given in App.~\ref{app:provenance}, the per-cell adjudication is specified as pseudocode in App.~\ref{app:pseudocode}, and the cell-by-cell coverage of the corpus is tabulated in App.~\ref{app:coverage}. Pool manifests, 1{,}028 per-cell JSONs (each carrying explicit \texttt{bh\_q\_value}, \texttt{adjudication\_\allowbreak pre/\allowbreak post\_\allowbreak fdr}, and a \texttt{bh\_batch\_hash} pinning its arm's BH-FDR batch), ensemble results with per-example predictions/confidences, the per-adapter logits cache for the $6$ pools where it was retained (L15), all analysis code, and the locked Phase-0 pre-registration are released at an anonymous repository.\footnote{\url{https://anonymous.4open.science/r/AdapterFrontier/} (de-anonymized at acceptance).} \texttt{scripts/\allowbreak reproduce\_\allowbreak paper\_\allowbreak numbers.\allowbreak py} re-derives every headline number ($286$ assertions, ${<}30$\,s) from JSON alone, including both frontier tables. We characterise this as \emph{JSON-level reproducibility} (every claim re-computable from the release); adapter \emph{weights} are available on request for the minority of pools where they were retained (L15), via the anonymous repository's issues and will carry archival hashes at acceptance.

\section*{Ethical Considerations}

\paragraph{Compute footprint.} Training the corpus pools and their baselines required approximately $1{,}500$ GPU-hours on a 50-node RTX 3060 cluster (orchestration in App.~\ref{app:cluster}), plus $\sim$$60$ A6000-hours for ensemble evaluation and serving benchmarks and $\sim$$85$ A6000-hours for the $361$ runs of the mechanism study and \S\ref{sec:a3} ($328$ on SNLI and Yahoo Answers, $33$ on MNLI). PEFT adapters are intentionally compute-light ($\sim$$1$--$8$ GPU-hours each); the adapter weights we retained (L15) are available on request via the anonymous repository so that those pools need not be retrained. Estimated CO\textsubscript{2}e: approximately $0.20$ tonnes (RTX 3060 at $170$\,W $\times$ $1{,}500$\,h $+$ A6000 at $300$\,W $\times$ $145$\,h, average grid intensity).

\paragraph{Dual use and misuse mitigation.} The adjudication framework is symmetric: it can report either direction (SUPPORTED or REVERSED) of an ensembling claim. We surface the $14.9\%$ REVERSED valid cells (pre-FDR; $13.2\%$ post-FDR at $q{=}0.05$) rather than filter them, and we discourage cherry-picking the $34.5\%$ SUPPORTED subset to manufacture a misleading positive headline. The released cell JSONs contain the full set of $\Delta$, CI, $p$-values, and adjudication labels precisely so that any selective citation is auditable.

\section{Statistical procedure details}\label{app:stats}

\paragraph{Paired bootstrap CI.} For each (pool, method, baseline) cell we compute $\Delta=\mu_{\text{ens}}-\mu_{\text{base}}$ on the test set ($N_{\text{test}}$ examples). To produce a $95\%$ CI we sample $B{=}5000$ bootstrap replicates: in each replicate we draw $N_{\text{test}}$ examples \emph{with replacement} and recompute $\Delta$ using the \emph{same} sampled indices for both ensemble and baseline. Reported CIs are the $2.5$ and $97.5$ percentiles of the $B$ replicates. Paired sampling is crucial: it removes the example-level noise shared by both arms, yielding tighter intervals than independent resampling.

\paragraph{$p$-values.} \emph{Accuracy}: the paired sign-flip test on $d_i=\mathbb{1}[\text{ens correct}]_i - \mathbb{1}[\text{base correct}]_i \in\{-1,0,+1\}$. Conditional on the $n_{10}{+}n_{01}$ discordant examples its exact null is $\mathrm{Binomial}(n_{10}{+}n_{01},\tfrac12)$, so we report the exact two-sided binomial $p$ (\texttt{scripts/\allowbreak apply\_\allowbreak bh\_\allowbreak valid\_\allowbreak exact.\allowbreak py}); earlier versions used a $10{,}000$-draw Monte Carlo estimate of the same quantity, kept under \texttt{legacy\_\allowbreak per\_\allowbreak arm\_\allowbreak mc}. \emph{ECE}: two-sided, by inverting the paired bootstrap of $\mathrm{ECE}_{\text{base}}-\mathrm{ECE}_{\text{ens}}$ ($B{=}5000$, same resampled indices in both arms, equal-mass bins rebuilt on every resample), $p=\min\{1,\,2\min(\Pr[\tilde\delta\leq0],\Pr[\tilde\delta\geq0])\}$, floored at $1/(B+1)$; ties count in both tails, which is what made $7$ cells reach $2$ before the cap. A sign-flip test does not apply to ECE, which is not a mean of per-example terms (L14).

\paragraph{Holm--Bonferroni correction.} Within each reported table (e.g., Table~\ref{tab:v11cells}), $p$-values across the $K$ cells are sorted ascending. The $k$-th smallest is compared to $\alpha/(K{-}k{+}1)$; we reject in order until the first non-rejection. This controls family-wise error rate at $\alpha{=}0.05$ across the entire table. The Holm-adjusted significance markers are denoted $^{*}$ in tables.

\paragraph{Bootstrap CI on $R^2_{\text{LOPO}}$.} The script that produced the CI in earlier versions was not preserved, and the paper described its resampling inconsistently, so we regenerated it with a stated procedure (\texttt{analysis/\allowbreak frontier\_\allowbreak cluster\_\allowbreak bootstrap.\allowbreak py}): resample the $48$ pools with replacement ($B{=}1{,}000$), keep every cell of each sampled pool, refit LOPO-CV with all copies of a pool in one fold, and take percentiles. The point estimate reproduces ($R^2_{\text{LOPO}}{=}{+}0.597$, $192$ cells) but the $95\%$ CI is $[{-}0.42, {+}0.75]$, much wider than the $[{+}0.30, {+}0.71]$ reported before; holding out a whole (model, task) group instead of one pool gives ${+}0.593$ over $25$ groups. The frontier is therefore a descriptive fit with wide uncertainty, which is how the paper now uses it (L1c).

\paragraph{Benjamini--Hochberg FDR, per arm.} Within-table Holm controls family-wise error at $\alpha{=}0.05$ for each reported table. For corpus-level multiplicity we additionally apply BH-FDR \citep{bh1995} at $q{=}0.05$, one batch per arm, over the $927$ valid cells only: $515$ accuracy cells (cutoff $p{\leq}0.0127$) and $412$ ECE cells ($p{\leq}0.028$); the $100$ HellaSwag cells and one smoke-test cell enter no batch and carry the verdict \texttt{excluded}. Pre-correction $320$ valid cells are SUPPORTED ($34.5\%$) and $138$ REVERSED ($14.9\%$); \textbf{post-FDR $258$ ($27.8\%$) and $122$ ($13.2\%$)} (accuracy $83/49$, ECE $175/73$; \texttt{analysis/\allowbreak bh\_\allowbreak valid\_\allowbreak exact\_\allowbreak summary.\allowbreak json}). A cell keeps its CI verdict only if it passes BH. Holm over a whole arm is now computable for accuracy (exact $p$; $19$ SUPPORTED and $25$ REVERSED of $515$); for ECE it stays unreachable, because Holm's first threshold $0.05/412$ is below the bootstrap's $1/5001$.

\paragraph{Independence robustness.} BH assumes independent or positively dependent $p$-values; cells sharing a pool are correlated. Re-running BH with one representative per (pool, baseline kind, metric) unit (\texttt{soft\_vote}), per arm over valid cells only, gives $129$ accuracy groups (cutoff $p{\leq}0.0143$) and $103$ ECE groups ($p{\leq}0.0356$): \textbf{$98$ of $232$ SUPPORTED ($42.2\%$), $18$ REVERSED ($7.8\%$)} (\texttt{analysis/\allowbreak independent\_\allowbreak group\_\allowbreak bh.\allowbreak py}). The per-cell REVERSED rate is higher mainly because \texttt{majority\_vote}, absent from the representatives, supplies $67$ of the $73$ valid ECE reversals. Pools that share a baseline and test set remain dependent after this step; the cluster-level analysis and Benjamini--Yekutieli counts in \S\ref{sec:findings} address that (\texttt{analysis/\allowbreak dependence\_\allowbreak robustness.\allowbreak py}).

\paragraph{Adjudication labels.} Each cell receives one of three labels: \textbf{supported} if the lower CI bound $>0$ \emph{and} the BH-FDR-adjusted $q$-value $<0.05$; \textbf{reversed} if the upper CI bound $<0$ \emph{and} $q{<}0.05$; \textbf{unsupported} otherwise (CI contains $0$, or $q{\geq}0.05$). The label is the cell's primary output and is what \emph{Figure~\ref{fig:verdict_landscape}} visualizes.

\section{Per-pool hyperparameter grids}\label{app:hp}
\paragraph{Pool-A (seed-only).} Fixed $r{=}8$, $\alpha{=}16$, $lr{=}3{\times}10^{-4}$, target$=$\{\texttt{q\_proj},\,\texttt{v\_proj}\}, dropout$=0.05$, epochs $=2$ (classification) or $3$ (causal LM); seed varied over $\{11, 21, \ldots, 201\}$ ($20$ adapters). Multi-choice (HellaSwag) uses the same HP via \texttt{AutoModelForMultipleChoice}.

\paragraph{Pool-B (HP-diverse).} $r{\in}\{4, 8, 16, 32\}$ $\times$ $\alpha{\in}\{8, 16, 32\}$ $\times$ $lr{\in}\{10^{-4}, 3{\times}10^{-4}\}$ $=$ $24$ adapters; seed fixed at $42$; other HP same as Pool-A.

\paragraph{Pool-C (method-mixed).} $\{\text{LoRA}, \text{DoRA}, \text{rsLoRA}, \text{PiSSA}\}$ $\times$ $5$ seeds $=$ $20$ adapters; $r{=}8, \alpha{=}16, lr{=}3{\times}10^{-4}$.

\paragraph{Baselines.} \emph{best\_of\_N} reuses Pool-A's first $N$ adapters; \emph{n\_rank} uses a separate sweep with $r{\in}\{16, 32\}$ $\times$ $epochs{\in}\{2, 4\}$ $\times$ $5$ seeds ($20$ adapters), then selects best-on-\texttt{val\_selection}; \emph{n\_steps} extends Pool-A's first adapter to $N{\times}$ epochs; \emph{n\_data} extends to $N{\times}$ training samples.

\section{Learned output weights, a LoRAHub-style proxy (record only)}\label{app:lorahub}
This is a \emph{proxy}, not LoRAHub \citep{huang2023lorahub}. LoRAHub composes the adapters' LoRA factors with signed weights into one module, chosen by gradient-free search, and serves that one module. Our proxy instead learns non-negative weights $\alpha\!\in\!\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^M$ on the members' output probabilities (L-BFGS-B on \texttt{val\_combine} cross-entropy) and still runs all $M$ members. It was run only on the $10$ HellaSwag pools, whose evaluator is not consistent across runs (L9), and it optimises on the split that evaluator also used for selection. We therefore record its per-pool numbers (\texttt{analysis/\allowbreak g3\_\allowbreak lorahub\_\allowbreak summary.\allowbreak json}) and draw no conclusion from them. An earlier version read a ``convergence regime'' rule into these pools; we withdraw it.

\section{What the ECE gain is made of}\label{app:ecequal}
Both computable from the same released cells (\texttt{analysis/\allowbreak ece\_\allowbreak test\_\allowbreak correction.\allowbreak py}). First, \emph{it is an ECE result, not a proper-score result}. On the identical $92$ cells the multiclass Brier improves in only $34$ (mean $\Delta{=}{-}0.0062$, i.e.\ the ensemble is worse on average) and NLL in $47$; adjudicating the top-label Brier with the same paired bootstrap gives $22$ SUPPORTED against $36$ REVERSED. ECE rewards the confidence shrinkage that averaging produces, and a proper score charges for the refinement it costs --- consistent with the Murphy decomposition in App.~\ref{app:mech}, where resolution gain is near-invariant. Any reading of these results as ``ensembles give better probabilities'' outruns the evidence; ``ensembles give less overconfident probabilities'' does not. Second, \emph{the gain belongs to probability averaging, not to ensembling as such}. Split by rule, ECE is SUPPORTED in $17/23$ pools for \texttt{soft\_vote}, $15/23$ for \texttt{logit\_avg} and $16/23$ for \texttt{greedy\_soup}, but only $3/23$ for \texttt{majority\_vote} --- which supplies $16$ of the $17$ reversals. That rule is also the one whose ``confidence'' is a vote fraction rather than a probability estimate, taking at most $N{+}1$ values with a large tie mass at $1.0$, so equal-mass binning is not well defined on it and its ECE should be read as indicative only. Excluding it leaves $48/69$ cells SUPPORTED (post-FDR); at the pool level (L1d's inferential unit) $14$ of $23$ pools are SUPPORTED on every probability-averaging rule.

Post hoc, on the six encoder corpus pools whose member logits survive (\texttt{analysis/\allowbreak temperature\_\allowbreak mechanism.\allowbreak py}). This appendix describes \emph{how} probability averaging changes calibration; it is not evidence that a single adapter can replace the population. The right-hand side below is the \emph{mean-logit model}, which still needs every member's forward pass, not a single adapter. The single-versus-population comparison, with both sides calibrated, is in \S\ref{sec:a3}, and on these six pools (Table~\ref{tab:tempctrl}) ECE differences after scaling both arms are not significant in any pool while NLL favours the ensemble in all six. For members' class-centred logits $z_i$, probability averaging is closely approximated by the mean-logit model at one temperature,
\begin{equation}
\tfrac{1}{M}\textstyle\sum_{i} \mathrm{softmax}(z_i) \;\approx\; \mathrm{softmax}(\bar z / T_{\mathrm{eq}}),
\end{equation}
the multiclass analogue of the probit approximation $\mathbb{E}[\sigma(z)]\approx\sigma\big(\mu/\sqrt{1+\pi s^2/8}\big)$ for $z\sim\mathcal{N}(\mu,s^2)$ \citep{mackay1992evidence}: averaging members whose logits disagree acts like a temperature that grows with their disagreement. We fit $T_{\mathrm{eq}}$ without labels, by minimising the KL divergence from the ensemble's probabilities, and it reproduces the ensemble's ECE to within $0.01$ in $5$ of $6$ pools (Table~\ref{tab:tempmech}).

An ensemble's calibration is therefore one point on its mean model's temperature curve, placed by how much the members disagree rather than by how much temperature the model needs ($T^*$, fitted on \texttt{val\_combine}). Averaging improves calibration over the mean model only when $T_{\mathrm{eq}}$ happens to move towards $T^*$, i.e.\ when $|\ln T^*-\ln T_{\mathrm{eq}}|<|\ln T^*|$, and this rule predicts whether it does in $5$ of $6$ pools. On ANLI the model needs $T^*{=}3.8$ but the ensemble behaves like $T{=}1.04$, so it stays overconfident (ECE $0.25$, against $0.06$ at $T^*$). On BoolQ and MNLI the mean model is already calibrated ($T^*\!\approx\!1$) and the ensemble's $T_{\mathrm{eq}}$ of $1.3$--$1.4$ makes it underconfident, so averaging \emph{raises} ECE. A fitted temperature aims at $T^*$ directly; averaging reaches it only by coincidence. The rule is a description of six pools, not a general result: $T_{\mathrm{eq}}$ moving towards $T^*$ in log distance is neither necessary nor sufficient for an ECE improvement in general, and with three or more classes probability averaging can reorder predictions in a way no temperature on $\bar z$ can. The six encoder pools are all the corpus logits we retained (L15); the six decoder pools in Table~\ref{tab:tempctrl} were rebuilt later and are not used for the $T_{\mathrm{eq}}$ analysis.

\section{Diversity--quality frontier (descriptive)}\label{app:scatter}
Everything in this appendix is descriptive. The prediction-space features were computed on cached logits without split indexing, so they include test examples, and the error-overlap feature uses the labels (L1c); a leak-free re-fit needs member logits that were not retained for these pools. Leave-one-pool-out CV holds out a pool, not its (model, task): pools sharing a baseline and test set stay on both sides, so the fit is not evidence of transfer to new models or tasks.

\paragraph{Frontier definition (formal).} For each cell let $y$ be the SUPPORTED-direction $\Delta$ (sign-aligned so positive = ensemble better) and $\phi\!\in\!\mathbb{R}^{d}$ the feature vector of App.~\ref{app:features}. The \emph{diversity-quality frontier} is $\hat y = f(\phi)$ fit by leave-one-pool-out CV. We fit three classes per slice under identical folds (ridge, random forest, gradient boosting; settings and all three scores in Table~\ref{tab:frontier_cross}) and report ridge --- the simplest --- everywhere, choosing the class before seeing the scores rather than per slice. On the headline slice ridge happens to also be the cross-class maximum ($R^2_{\text{LOPO}}{=}{+}0.597$ vs.\ ${+}0.276$ and ${+}0.285$, Table~\ref{tab:frontier_strat}); on the other three it is not, so the fixed choice costs us $R^2$ on three of four slices rather than buying it on any. CIs are percentile cluster bootstraps resampling \emph{pools} (App.~\ref{app:stats}). Note the sign: encoder $\Delta$ is predominantly negative and decoder $\Delta$ positive (\S\ref{sec:findings}), so on encoders this regression tracks \emph{how much a pool stands to lose}, descriptively (L1c). Because the families differ in sign, we checked that it is not merely telling them apart: family alone reaches $R^2_{\text{LOPO}}{=}{+}0.20$ on the same folds and adding it to the features lowers $R^2$ to ${+}0.57$, so diversity carries ${+}0.39$ beyond the split (\texttt{frontier\_\allowbreak family\_\allowbreak baseline.\allowbreak py}).

\section{Negative mechanism results (demoted from \S\ref{sec:findings})}\label{app:mech}

\paragraph{Summary.} Three candidate explanations for the family and metric structure of \S\ref{sec:findings} do \emph{not} survive testing, and we report them as open problems rather than findings. (i) Decoder $H(c|\mathrm{conf})$ is $21\%$ lower ($0.437$ vs.\ $0.556$), but $0.110$ of that $0.119$ gap is the decoders' higher accuracy, not sharper confidence (item (i) below), and it predicts no calibration benefit. (ii) Linear mode connectivity does not predict ensemble effectiveness (per-pool barriers in App.~\ref{app:lmc}): factor-space barriers span $+0.91$ to $+0.02$ across four pools with no corresponding pattern, which four pools cannot turn into a general claim. (iii) The accuracy-specificity of the frontier is metric-general --- ECE ($R^2{=}{-}0.06$) and Brier ($-0.30$) are unpredictable while NLL ($+0.42$) and MCE ($+0.22$) are --- and the natural refinement-versus-reliability explanation is falsified: under the Murphy decomposition, resolution gain is near-invariant ($\sigma$ $0.21{\times}$ accuracy's) and uncorrelated with accuracy gain ($r{=}{-}0.10$).

Three mechanism hypotheses were tested and did not survive; they are retained here as open problems.

\paragraph{(i) Confidence--correctness separation.} Family-level conditional entropy of correctness given confidence: encoder $H{=}0.556$, $I{=}0.097$ ($n{=}29$ pools); decoder $H{=}0.437$, $I{=}0.106$ ($n{=}25$). Decoder $H$ is $21\%$ lower, but that is not evidence of sharper discrimination: since $H(c){=}H(c\,|\,\mathrm{conf}){+}I$, the marginal entropies are $0.653$ (encoder) and $0.543$ (decoder), so $0.110$ of the $0.119$ gap in $H$ is the decoders' higher accuracy and only $0.009$ is information carried by confidence. An earlier version read the gap as a higher-SNR signal caused by architecture; we withdraw that reading. DeBERTa-v3 ($n{=}1$) is excluded from the family summary; its per-pool $H{=}0.940$ appears in App.~\ref{app:features}. This gap does not yield a predictable calibration benefit (item iii).

\paragraph{(ii) Linear mode connectivity.} Across four pools (BERT-base Pool-A/C, Qwen-0.5B Pool-A, DeBERTa-v3 Pool-A on MNLI) the factor-space barrier ranges from $+0.91$ to $+0.02$, and the ordering of ensemble gains does not follow it (App.~\ref{app:lmc}); with four pools and five pairs each this is an observation, not a test.

\paragraph{(iii) Refinement is not the mechanism.} The ECE non-result is not an estimator artifact. Recomputing the \texttt{n\_rank} LOPO regression from released per-adapter scalar metrics (point $R^2$; proper-score paired CIs require per-example probabilities and are deferred), accuracy gain is predictable ($R^2{=}{+}0.60$) but neither ECE ($-0.06$) nor Brier ($-0.30$). The split is not accuracy-versus-calibration: NLL ($+0.42$) and MCE ($+0.22$) \emph{are} predictable, tracking a metric's weight on the confident/tail region rather than its calibration label. The natural explanation --- that predictability localizes to the refinement rather than the reliability component of a proper score --- is falsified: under the Murphy decomposition of the confidence-Brier, resolution gain is near-invariant across the \texttt{n\_rank} comparison ($\sigma$ $0.21{\times}$ that of accuracy) and uncorrelated with accuracy gain ($r{=}{-}0.10$). Accuracy predictability does not flow through the calibration geometry; why the frontier is accuracy-specific remains open.

\section{Distillation $\alpha\times T$ sweep (Qwen-0.5B MNLI)}\label{app:distill}
We sweep the soft-target distillation HPs $\alpha\in\{0.5,0.7,0.9\}$ (weight on soft-target loss vs.\ hard-target) and $T\in\{1,2,4,8\}$ (softmax temperature on teacher logits) for a $3\times4$ grid (12 settings) on Pool-A Qwen-0.5B MNLI. Vanilla single (no distillation): $0.8365 / 0.0465$ (acc/ECE). Soft-vote ensemble target: $0.8495 / 0.0187$. Best student: $(\alpha{=}0.9, T{=}4)$ yields $0.8479 / 0.0397$, recovering $87\%$ of the soft-vote accuracy gain at $1\times$ student cost. Across the grid, accuracy varies $\pm0.003$ and ECE varies $\pm0.012$; the sweep is locally robust. The same sweep on Pool-A BERT-base MNLI yields a student strictly worse than vanilla single on both arms, consistent with the encoder-accuracy-REVERSED finding (no signal to distill).

\section{Frontier regression features}\label{app:features}
For each (pool, method, baseline) cell we extract a feature vector $\phi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$ used to fit the diversity-quality frontier. Features are grouped:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{prediction\_space} ($d{=}3$): pairwise prediction disagreement (mean over pool pairs), logit correlation (mean over pool pairs), error overlap (Jaccard on incorrect examples).
\item \textbf{parameter\_space} ($d{=}3$): mean weight cosine distance, mean centered-kernel-alignment (CKA) on penultimate layer, weight $L_2$ norm spread.
\item \textbf{HP entropy} ($d{=}2$): entropy of $r$-distribution within pool, entropy of $\{lr, \alpha\}$ joint distribution.
\item \textbf{ensemble quality} ($d{=}2$): pool mean val\_acc, pool best-single val\_acc.
\end{itemize}
Per-feature ablation on the encoder \texttt{acc vs n\_rank} slice: dropping the prediction\_space group crashes $R^2$ from $+0.62$ to $-24.5$ ($\Delta R^2 {=}{+}25.0$); dropping parameter\_space drops $R^2$ by $0.56$; dropping HP-entropy \emph{improves} $R^2$ by $0.08$ (HP entropy adds in-sample fit but no out-of-pool signal). A single-group ``only prediction\_space'' model achieves $R^2{=}0.634$, \emph{higher} than the full feature set.

\section{Cell adjudication pseudocode}\label{app:pseudocode}
Mirrors \texttt{analysis/\allowbreak compute\_\allowbreak match.\allowbreak py} and \texttt{scripts/\allowbreak apply\_\allowbreak bh\_\allowbreak valid\_\allowbreak exact.\allowbreak py}; ECE is equal-mass, $15$ bins.
{\scriptsize\begin{verbatim}
def adjudicate(ens, base, gold, metric):
  # ens/base: per-example (pred, conf) on test
  ca = (ens.pred == gold).astype(float)
  cb = (base.pred == gold).astype(float)
  N = len(gold)
  if metric == 'acc':
    stat = lambda i: ca[i].mean() - cb[i].mean()
  else:  # positive = ensemble better calibrated
    stat = lambda i: (ECE(base.conf[i], cb[i])
                      - ECE(ens.conf[i], ca[i]))
  delta = stat(np.arange(N))       # point estimate
  boot = np.array([stat(randint(0, N, N))
                   for _ in range(5000)])
  ci_lo, ci_hi = np.quantile(boot, [.025, .975])
  if metric == 'acc':   # exact sign-flip null
    n10 = int((ca > cb).sum())
    n01 = int((ca < cb).sum())
    p = binomtest(n10, n10 + n01, 0.5).pvalue
  else:                 # invert bootstrap
    # separate B=5000 resample, seeded per cell
    p = 2 * min((boot <= 0).mean(),
                (boot >= 0).mean())
    p = min(1.0, max(p, 1 / 5001))
  if   ci_lo > 0: label = 'supported'
  elif ci_hi < 0: label = 'reversed'
  else:           label = 'unsupported'
  return delta, (ci_lo, ci_hi), p, label

# then, separately for the accuracy and ECE arms,
# over valid cells only (no HellaSwag, no smoke test):
#   q = BH(p over every valid cell of the arm)
#   post_fdr = label if q <= 0.05 else 'unsupported'
\end{verbatim}}

\section{Cluster orchestration details}\label{app:cluster}
The 50-node RTX 3060 12GB cluster is orchestrated via \texttt{sweep\_runner.py}, an in-house SSH-based fan-out engine. Each sweep config (JSON) declares a Cartesian parameter grid; the runner stages source files, assigns one (param-combination, node) pair per slot, and tracks per-job exit codes in \texttt{sweep\_\allowbreak runs/\allowbreak <name>/\allowbreak results.\allowbreak json}. Pool-A sweeps complete in $\sim$4--8 GPU-hours wall clock (parallelism factor $\sim$20); Pool-B/C in $\sim$16--24 hours. The v1.2 A6000 sweeps (Qwen-3B, Qwen-7B, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct on GSM8K) use \texttt{local\_\allowbreak gpu\_\allowbreak runner.\allowbreak py}, a sequential per-seed launcher pinned to a single GPU index, suitable for models that do not fit on the 12GB cluster. Adapter weights are pulled back via \texttt{collect\_\allowbreak remote\_\allowbreak adapters.\allowbreak py} and assembled into pool manifests via \texttt{build\_\allowbreak adapter\_\allowbreak pool.\allowbreak py}; the manifest schema is documented in \texttt{scripts/\allowbreak validate\_\allowbreak pool.\allowbreak py}.
